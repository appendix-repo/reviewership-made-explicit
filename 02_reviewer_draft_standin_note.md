# Stand-in note on the reviewer draft (run 02)

To the orchestrating session. This note is not part of the review. It records where I, a model standing in for the focal reviewer, may depart from what a human reviewer would produce in one sitting of about an hour without the workbench.

## 1. What I noticed but left out

I left these out either because this reviewer would plausibly not have found or checked them in the hour, or because there was no room for them in about 1,000 words.

- **A second discriminating test of the rival.** Mimetic theory, as the paper itself states it in §1, predicts that imitation strengthens under uncertainty. So a peer × uncertainty interaction would be a discriminating test. The paper's uncertainty measure would not serve, though. Admissions volatility is a transaction-cost "disturbance", not uncertainty about whether a practice works. I cut this for length, not because the reviewer would miss it.
- **Construct fit of the uncertainty measure.** I did not discuss whether admissions volatility creates the IT adaptation needs the model assumes. Uncertainty is a main regressor for H2, so std:7(a) arguably applies, and Mode C may record it as a skip.
- **Spending weights move with sourcing.** The cost of a bought function (p − s + hold-up) differs from the cost of a made one (p + G). So Φ is not a fixed-weight share of functions, and the aggregation problem goes beyond the density point the draft makes.
- **The corner case.** The threshold k* can exceed 1, at which point every function is outsourced. This matters for aggregation; omitted.
- **Uncertainty at mean modularity.** Modularity is not centred, so the uncertainty coefficient in Model 3 is the effect at m = 0. At mean modularity (0.46), the implied effect is about +0.056. The draft cites only the m = 0 coefficient and does not explain this.
- **Within R² in Model 4.** It jumps from 0.14 to 0.19 when staffing is added, which is consistent with the mechanical link the draft criticises. Omitted.
- **Robustness checks not shown.** The paper claims robustness for a share that includes licences, and for non-system hospitals, but shows neither. Omitted as minor.
- **Never-treated states (std:10(d)).** Whether any states had not adopted a requirement by 2019 is covered only implicitly, through "not-yet-treated".
- **Loh and Venkatraman (1992).** Their paper on the "Kodak effect" in the diffusion of IT outsourcing is from my own recall. I left it out because it is less directly a study that adjudicates between the two accounts, and this reviewer is less likely to know it.
- **Open decisions in the specification.** It also asks the reviewer to confirm two recording choices and to confirm the specification as final. I settled only bs:T7, as instructed, and answered neither of the others.
