**bs:E3. My concerns.**
I have two, both from one quick read.
1. **Do the model and the empirics actually connect? (C1 and C2.)** The model gives an outsourcing threshold for individual functions, but the outcome measured is a share of spending. The uncertainty result also goes the wrong way. H2 predicts a stronger modularity effect when uncertainty is high. Table 2 (Model 3) shows a negative interaction, which the authors call "contrary to H2", and §5 explains it with something outside the model. I want to know whether that's a failed prediction, a problem in the model or a problem in the test. This concern is about the theory and the evidence together.
2. **Is the dismissal of the institutional account fair? (C3, C4, and the "rule out" wording in the abstract and §5.)** Part of this is about how the rival is represented. Mimicry is tested with a single measure: the lagged mean outsourcing of other sample hospitals in the same state (§3.2). Part is about evidence. A peer coefficient that loses significance once modularity is added is not obviously enough to rule anything out. And part is about wording. It also sits oddly that §5 explains the H2 result with legitimacy pressures, an institutional mechanism, in a paper that claims to have ruled that account out. The second reviewer will speak to institutional theory. From this reviewer I want the evidence side.

Settled, so don't spend effort there: the question matters, and the mechanism deserves to be taken seriously. A long, balanced panel is a real asset, though the authors acknowledge in §5.2 that requiring a response in every year may skew the sample. C5 needs little effort (see E1).
